Question 2. Two Light Spring Balances A and B in Series Supporting a 4 kg Block

Let's break this down from the light spring balance concept — this question is mainly testing whether you understand that light spring balances have negligible mass ($m = 0$), so internal tension is uniform through connected series links.

What to do first: A block of mass $4\text{ kg}$ is suspended by two light spring balances A and B (usually one below the other in series, or supporting each other).

1. How to analyze the lower spring balance

Suppose balance B is directly holding the $4\text{ kg}$ block. * For balance B to hold a $4\text{ kg}$ block in equilibrium (or at rest), the spring inside B must experience a tension equal to the weight of the block: $$ W = m g = 4 \times g = 4\text{ kg wt} $$ * So balance B reads $4\text{ kg}$.

2. How to analyze the upper spring balance (Balance A)

Since both balances are light (negligible mass, $m_{\text{spring}} = 0$): * Consider balance B itself: it has negligible mass, so the net force on balance B is zero ($m_{\text{spring}} a = 0$). * The upward pull on B from A must balance the downward pull on B from the $4\text{ kg}$ block. * Therefore, the tension pulling on balance A is also equal to the weight of the $4\text{ kg}$ block ($4\text{ kg wt}$). * So balance A also reads $4\text{ kg}$.

Try it: If balances had mass, tensions could differ. But because they are light, what does $\sum F = m_{\text{spring}} a$ tell you about the tension above and below each spring?

$$ \text{Balance A} = 4\text{ kg},\quad \text{Balance B} = 4\text{ kg} $$

Final answers

Compare with the options (A, B, C, D):

$$ \text{Readings: } 4\text{ kg and } 4\text{ kg} \quad \textbf{(Option C)} $$

Self-check: A light spring balance simply measures the tension in the string passing through/supporting it. Since string tension is uniform throughout a light string/spring system holding a static $4\text{ kg}$ mass, both show $4\text{ kg}$.

Question 1. Spring Balance Reading with a Moving Pulley (Atwood Machine)

Let's break this down from the suspension tension idea — this question is mainly testing whether you know what a spring balance actually measures (the upward force holding the pulley, which is equal to the two downward tensions from the string).

What to do first: Recall that a spring balance measures the force pulling on its hook. Here, the frictionless pulley of negligible weight is suspended from the balance, and the string holding $m_1 = 1\text{ kg}$ and $m_2 = 5\text{ kg}$ passes over it.

1. How to find the string tension $T$

Key idea: This is our standard Atwood machine! You already know how to find the tension $T$ in the string connecting two masses $m_1$ and $m_2$.

Recall formula for tension $T$: $$ T = \frac{2 m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2} \, g $$

Try it: Substitute $m_1 = 1\text{ kg}$ and $m_2 = 5\text{ kg}$ into this formula. What do you get for $T$ in terms of $g$?

$$ T = \frac{2(1)(5)}{1+5}\,g = \frac{10}{6}\,g = \frac{5}{3}\,g $$

2. What force does the spring balance experience?

What to do: Look at the forces pulling down on the suspended pulley.

  • The string goes up over the pulley on both sides.
  • Each side exerts a tension $T$ pulling downward on the axle of the pulley.
  • Since the pulley is massless, the spring balance must support both downward tensions:

$$ F_{\text{spring}} = T + T = 2T $$

Try it: Multiply your tension $T = \frac{5}{3}g$ by 2. What total force does the spring balance read?

$$ F_{\text{spring}} = 2 \left( \frac{5}{3}g \right) = \frac{10}{3}\,g \implies \frac{10}{3}\text{ kg wt} $$

Final answers

Compare with the options (A, B, C, D):

$$ \text{Reading} = \frac{10}{3}\text{ kg wt} \quad \textbf{(Option B)} $$

Self-check: Notice $2T$ is less than $(m_1+m_2)g = 6\,g$ because the masses are accelerating, relieving some of the static load on the ceiling/spring balance.

Illustration 12. Three Identical Blocks $A,B,C$ — How to Find $F$, Tensions, and $a$

Let's break this down from the connected-system idea — this question is mainly testing whether you can correctly use same acceleration for all blocks and isolate each block.

What to do first: All three blocks are linked by light, inextensible strings $\implies$ they move together with same $a$ to the right. A light string has same tension throughout, but $T_{AB}$ and $T_{BC}$ are different. The measured $T_{AB}=8.0\text{ N}$ is your key — start from the block that has only one force.

1. How to link tension to acceleration

Key idea: For any block, $\sum F = ma$ to the right.

Look at block $C$ (leftmost, simplest — only one horizontal force).

Forces on $C$: $T_{BC}$ to the right, nothing else (smooth floor, no friction).

So for $C$: $$ T_{BC} = m\,a \tag{always true} $$

Try it: You don't know $T_{BC}$ yet, so also look at the pair $B+C$ together. What forces pull $B+C$ to the right?

Hint: $B+C$ together are pulled only by $T_{AB}=8.0\text{ N}$ (the string $A!-!B$ drags $B$, $B$ drags $C$). So for system $B+C$: $$ T_{AB} = (m_B + m_C)a = (2.0+2.0)a $$

Horizontal component: Here horizontally the net force on $B+C$ is just $T_{AB}$.

$$ T_{AB} = (m_B+m_C)a \implies 8.0 = 4.0\,a $$

This tells you $a$ immediately — try solving for $a$ before scrolling.

$$ a = \frac{8.0}{4.0}=2.0\text{ m/s}^2 $$

This $a$ is the same for $A$, $B$, and $C$.

2. How to find $T_{BC}$ (string $B$–$C$)

What to do: Now that you have $a$, go back to block $C$ alone: $T_{BC}=ma$.

So: $$ T_{BC}=m_C\,a = 2.0\times 2.0 $$

Try: multiply — what do you get?

$$ T_{BC}=4.0\text{ N} $$

This pushes block $C$ to the right. Notice $T_{BC}<T_{AB}$ — it only has to drag $C$, while $T_{AB}$ drags $B+C$.

Check via $B$: $T_{AB}-T_{BC}=ma \implies 8.0-4.0=4.0=2.0\times2.0$ ✓

3. How to find the pull $F$

What to do: Isolate block $A$ (rightmost, where $F$ acts).

Forces on $A$: $F$ to the right, $T_{AB}=8.0\text{ N}$ to the left.

$$ F - T_{AB}= m_A\,a $$

So: $$ F = T_{AB}+m_A a = 8.0 + 2.0\times 2.0 $$

Try: add them.

$$ F = 12\text{ N} $$

Alternative (whole system check): $A+B+C$ total mass $=6.0\text{ kg}$, so $F = (m_A+m_B+m_C)a =6.0\times2.0=12\text{ N}$ — same result. Use this to verify.

Final answers

Try writing them yourself first, then compare:

$$ a = 2.0\text{ m/s}^2\text{ for all blocks (to the right)} $$
$$ T_{BC}=4.0\text{ N},\quad T_{AB}=8.0\text{ N (given)} $$
$$ F = 12\text{ N} $$

Self-check: Is $T_{BC}<T_{AB}<F$? Yes $4<8<12$. Does $F =6a$? $12=6\times2$ ✓. If you got a different $a$, re-examine which mass $T_{AB}$ is pulling.

Two Blocks Connected by a Massive Uniform String on a Smooth Floor — Pulled by $F$

Problem: Two blocks $A$ and $B$ of masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ (let $A$ = pulled block $=m_1$, $B$ = rear block $=m_2$; if swapped, interchange $m_1\leftrightarrow m_2$) are connected by a uniform string of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ lying on a smooth (frictionless) horizontal floor. Block $A$ is pulled by a constant horizontal force $F$ as in the figure ($B$ — string — $A \rightarrow F$). The string also lies on the floor.

(a) Find the common acceleration $a$ and the tensions $T_A$ (at the $A$-end) and $T_B$ (at the $B$-end).
(b) Find tension $T(x)$ at a distance $x$ from the rear block $B$ ($0\le x\le\ell$), in terms of $T_A, T_B, m, \ell, x$.

Step 1: Identify the System & Constraints

  • Smooth floor: No friction, normal cancels weight, only horizontal force is $F$.
  • Inextensible string: String does not stretch $\implies$ $A$, every element of the string, and $B$ have the same acceleration $a$ (to the right) and same velocity at any instant.
  • Uniform massive string: Linear mass density is constant: $$ \lambda = \frac{m}{\ell} \qquad \left[\frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}}\right], \qquad dm = \lambda\,dx $$
  • Massive vs. massless: If $m=0$, tension would be uniform. With $m>0$, tension must vary along the string to accelerate the string's own mass.

Total inertial mass being pulled: $M_{\text{tot}} = m_1 + m_2 + m$.

Step 2: Acceleration of the Whole System — Treat as One Body

Consider $A$ + string + $B$ as a single system. External horizontal force is only $F$.

$$ F = M_{\text{tot}} \, a $$

$$ a = \frac{F}{m_1 + m_2 + m} \tag{★} $$

Direction: to the right (along $F$). All parts share this $a$. If $m=0$, this reduces to the familiar $a = F/(m_1+m_2)$.

Check: If $m_1\leftrightarrow m_2$ (i.e. $A$ is $m_2$), replace $m_1+m_2$ symmetrically — $a$ is unchanged.

Step 3: Tensions at the Ends — Isolate the Blocks

At the rear block $B$ (mass $m_2$): Only horizontal force is $T_B$ to the right (string pulls $B$).

$$ T_B = m_2 \, a $$

With (★): $$ T_B = \frac{m_2}{m_1+m_2+m}\,F \tag{1} $$

At the front block $A$ (mass $m_1$): Pulled forward by $F$, backward by $T_A$.

$$ F - T_A = m_1 a \implies T_A = F - m_1 a $$

$$ T_A = F\left(1 - \frac{m_1}{M_{\text{tot}}}\right) = \frac{m_2 + m}{M_{\text{tot}}}\,F \tag{2} $$

Alternatively, consider $B$ + string as a subsystem: $T_A = (m_2 + m) a$, same result.

$$ T_A = \frac{m_2 + m}{m_1+m_2+m}\,F, \qquad T_B = \frac{m_2}{m_1+m_2+m}\,F $$

Inequality: $T_A > T_B$ always ($T_A - T_B = m a$ is needed to accelerate the string). If string were massless ($m=0$), $T_A = T_B$.

If the problem labels $A=m_2$, $B=m_1$, just swap: $T_A = (m_1+m)F/M_{\text{tot}}$, $T_B = m_1F/M_{\text{tot}}$.

Step 4: How Tension Varies Along a Massive String

Take a cut at distance $x$ from $B$ ($x=0$ at $B$-end, $x=\ell$ at $A$-end). Let $T(x)$ be the internal tension there, acting to the right on the left subsystem.

Isolate the left subsystem: $B$ + string segment of length $x$ (mass $\lambda x = m x/\ell$).

Mass of left part: $$ M(x) = m_2 + \lambda x = m_2 + \frac{m x}{\ell} $$

The only horizontal external force on this isolated left part is $T(x)$ (to the right), so:

$$ T(x) = M(x)\, a = \left(m_2 + \frac{m x}{\ell}\right)a \tag{3} $$

Verify endpoints: * $x=0$: $T(0)=m_2 a = T_B$ ✓ * $x=\ell$: $T(\ell)=(m_2+m)a = T_A$ ✓

Differential view: $dT = \lambda\,a\,dx \implies \frac{dT}{dx}=\lambda a$ = constant, so $T$ varies linearly with $x$.

Step 5: Express $T(x)$ Through $T_A, T_B$

From (1)–(2): $T_A - T_B = m a$, so $a = (T_A - T_B)/m$ and $\lambda a = (T_A-T_B)/\ell$.

Insert into (3): $$ T(x) = m_2 a + \lambda a x = T_B + \lambda a x $$

$$ T(x) = T_B + \frac{x}{\ell}\,(T_A - T_B) \tag{★ ★} $$

This is the required linear interpolation:

$$ T(x) = T_B + (T_A - T_B)\frac{x}{\ell} = T_A - (T_A - T_B)\frac{\ell - x}{\ell} $$

Using $F$ explicitly (with $B=m_2$): $$ T(x) = \frac{F}{M_{\text{tot}}}\left(m_2 + \frac{m x}{\ell}\right) $$

If you measure $x'$ from the front ($A$-end), $x'=\ell-x$, then $T = T_A - (T_A-T_B)x'/\ell$.

Step 6: Checks & Special Cases

  • Massless string limit $m\to0$: $T_A = T_B = m_2F/(m_1+m_2)$, and (★★) gives $T(x)=T_A$ constant ✓
  • No rear block $m_2=0$: $T_B=0$, $T_A=mF/M_{\text{tot}}$, $T(x)=T_A\,x/\ell$ — triangular profile, zero at free end ✓
  • Dimensions: $[T]=\text{N}$, $x/\ell$ dimensionless ✓
  • Graph: $T$ vs $x$ is a straight line from $(0,T_B)$ to $(\ell,T_A)$.

Final Answers

  • (a)

    $$ \mathbf{a = \frac{F}{m_1+m_2+m}\ \text{(to the right)}} $$

    $$ \mathbf{T_B = \frac{m_2 F}{m_1+m_2+m}},\qquad \mathbf{T_A = \frac{(m_2+m)F}{m_1+m_2+m}} $$

    (Swap $m_1\leftrightarrow m_2$ if your $A=m_2$, $B=m_1$.)

  • (b)

    $$ \mathbf{T(x) = T_B + \frac{x}{\ell}\,(T_A - T_B) = \left(m_2+\frac{m x}{\ell}\right)\frac{F}{m_1+m_2+m}} $$

    for $x$ measured from $B$ ($0$ at $B$, $\ell$ at $A$). Linear variation, slope $\lambda a$.
Mnemonic: Tension is larger nearer the pull — it must drag more mass behind it. The diagram of $T$ vs $x$ is the same as the mass-behind profile.

Illustration 14. Massive string Machine — Released from Rest, $m_2 > m_1$

Problem: The system shown in the figure (two blocks $m_1$ and $m_2$ hanging vertically from a single pulley) is released from rest. Assuming $m_2 > m_1$, find the accelerations of the blocks and the tension in the string.

Step 1: Understand the System & Constraints

  • System: Ideal Massive string machine. Blocks $m_1$ (left) and $m_2$ (right) connected by a string over a fixed pulley.
  • Key Constraints (Ideal Case):
    • String massless & inextensible $\implies$ Tension $T$ is uniform and the length constraint $y_1 + y_2 = \text{constant}$ gives: $$ v_1 = -v_2, \quad a_1 = -a_2 \implies |a_1| = |a_2| = a $$
    • Pulley massless & frictionless $\implies$ No rotational inertia, $T_{\text{left}} = T_{\text{right}} = T$.
    • Released from rest $\implies$ $u=0$, so $s = \frac{1}{2}at^2$ later, but now we only need $a$.

Since $m_2 > m_1$, gravity dominates on the right. So $m_2$ accelerates downward and $m_1$ accelerates upward with the same magnitude $a$.

As the textbook states: "It obvious that both blocks move with same acceleration magnitudes. Say it is 'a'. Since $m_2$ is heavier, it moves downwards and $m_1$ moves upwards."

Step 2: Draw Free-Body Diagrams (FBD)

FBD for $m_1$ (taking upward as $+ve$): * Upward force: $T$ * Downward force: $m_1 g$ (weight) * Net force upward must provide $m_1 a$:

$$ T - m_1 g = m_1 a \tag{1} $$

FBD for $m_2$ (taking downward as $+ve$): * Downward force: $m_2 g$ * Upward force: $T$ * Net force downward must provide $m_2 a$:

$$ m_2 g - T = m_2 a \tag{2} $$

Intuition: Net driving force for the whole system = weight difference $(m_2 - m_1)g$. Total mass being accelerated = $m_1 + m_2$.

Step 3: Solve for Acceleration $a$

Add equation (1) and (2) to eliminate $T$:

$$ (T - m_1 g) + (m_2 g - T) = m_1 a + m_2 a $$

$$ m_2 g - m_1 g = (m_1 + m_2) a $$

$$ (m_2 - m_1)g = (m_1 + m_2)a $$

$$ a = \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2} \, g $$

Direction: $\vec{a_1} = a \, \hat{y}$ (up), $\vec{a_2} = a \, (-\hat{y})$ (down). Magnitude is as above.

If you use $M = m_2$ and $m = m_1$ (as in your handwritten note), this is: $$ a = \frac{M - m}{M + m} g $$

Step 4: Solve for Tension $T$

Use $a$ in equation (1): $T = m_1(g + a)$

$$ T = m_1 g + m_1 \left( \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2} g \right) = m_1 g \left[ 1 + \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2} \right] $$

$$ T = m_1 g \left[ \frac{(m_1 + m_2) + (m_2 - m_1)}{m_1 + m_2} \right] = m_1 g \left[ \frac{2m_2}{m_1 + m_2} \right] $$

$$ T = \frac{2 m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2} \, g $$

Check with equation (2): $T = m_2(g - a) = m_2 g \left(1 - \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\right) = \frac{2m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}g$ — same result.

Step 5: Verification & Limiting Cases

  • Dimensional check: $[a] = m/s^2$, $[T] = kg \cdot m/s^2 = N$ ✓
  • Equal masses: If $m_1 = m_2 = m$, then $a = 0$ and $T = mg$ — system in equilibrium, as expected.
  • One mass negligible: If $m_1 \ll m_2$, then $a \approx g$, $T \approx 2m_1 g$ — heavy mass falls almost freely.
  • Inequality: Always $m_1 g < T < m_2 g$ when $m_2 > m_1$.

Final Answer

$$ \mathbf{a = \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2} g \quad \text{(magnitude)}} $$ $$ \mathbf{T = \frac{2 m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2} g} $$

With $g \approx 9.8 \, m/s^2$ (or $10 \, m/s^2$ for approximation). Example: if $m_1 = 2\,kg$, $m_2 = 4\,kg$: $a = \frac{1}{3}g \approx 3.27\,m/s^2$, $T \approx 26.1\,N$.

Note on your handwriting: The step $T - mg = ma \implies T = ma + mg$ and then $Mg - ma - mg = Ma$ is exactly the elimination above. Just keep notation consistent ($m_1, m_2$ throughout) for full marks.

14. Massive string Machine — Two Blocks Hanging from a Massless Pulley

Problem Statement: The system shown in the figure is released from rest. Assuming $m_2 > m_1$, find the accelerations of the blocks and the tension in the string. It is given that both blocks move with same acceleration magnitude $a$, and $m_2$ (heavier) moves downwards while $m_1$ moves upwards.

Verification of Your Handwritten Work: Your derivation $a = \dfrac{g(M-m)}{M+m}$ (with $M=m_2$, $m=m_1$) is correct for the acceleration. The steps $T-mg=ma$, $Mg-T=Ma$, $T=m(a+g)$ and $Mg-ma-mg=Ma$ are all valid under ideal pulley assumptions. Full credit for $a$ — below is the complete solution including tension and formal checks.

Step 1: Assumptions & Kinematics

  • Pulley: massless, frictionless (moment of inertia $I=0$, no friction at axle)
  • String: massless ($\mu = 0$) and inextensible ($L = \text{const}$)
  • Hence tension $T$ is uniform throughout the string, and inextensibility gives constraint:

$$ |a_1| = |a_2| = a, \quad |v_1| = |v_2|, \quad s_1 + s_2 = \text{const} $$

  • Released from rest: $v(0)=0$, so motion is uniformly accelerated.
  • Choose sign convention: $+ve$ upward for $m_1$, $+ve$ downward for $m_2$ (along direction of motion).

Step 2: Free-Body Diagrams

For $m_1$ (lighter, moves up): Forces: Tension $T$ upward, weight $m_1 g$ downward. Net upward force = $T - m_1 g$.

For $m_2$ (heavier, moves down): Forces: Weight $m_2 g$ downward, tension $T$ upward. Net downward force = $m_2 g - T$.

Diagram: Pulley fixed at top, $m_1$ on left, $m_2$ on right. Both hang vertically.

Step 3: Equations of Motion ($F_{net}=ma$)

For $m_1$: $$ T - m_1 g = m_1 a \tag{1} $$

For $m_2$: $$ m_2 g - T = m_2 a \tag{2} $$

This matches your handwritten equations $T-mg=ma$ and $Mg-T=Ma$.

From (1): $$ T = m_1(g + a) \tag{3} $$

And from (2): $$ T = m_2(g - a) \tag{4} $$

Step 4: Solve for Acceleration $a$

Add (1) + (2) to eliminate $T$:

$$ (T - m_1 g) + (m_2 g - T) = m_1 a + m_2 a $$

$$ (m_2 - m_1)g = (m_1 + m_2)a $$

$$ a = \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\, g $$

In your notation $M=m_2$, $m=m_1$:

$$ a = \frac{M - m}{M + m}\, g \quad \checkmark $$

  • Vector form: $\vec{a_1} = +a\,\hat{j}$ (up), $\vec{a_2} = -a\,\hat{j}$ (down) if $\hat{j}$ is up.
  • Physical intuition: $a = \dfrac{\text{net driving force}}{\text{total inertial mass}} = \dfrac{(m_2-m_1)g}{m_1+m_2}$.

Step 5: Solve for Tension $T$

Substitute $a$ into (3):

$$ T = m_1 g \left(1 + \frac{m_2-m_1}{m_1+m_2}\right) = m_1 g \left(\frac{m_1+m_2 + m_2 - m_1}{m_1+m_2}\right) $$

$$ T = \frac{2m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\, g $$

Equivalently from (4): $T = m_2(g-a)$ gives same result. In $M,m$ notation:

$$ T = \frac{2Mm}{M+m}\, g $$

Check: $m_1 g < T < m_2 g$ always holds when $m_2 > m_1$, as required for $m_1$ to accelerate up and $m_2$ to accelerate down.

Step 6: Dimensional & Limiting-Case Checks

  • Dimensions: $[a]=LT^{-2}$, $[T]=MLT^{-2}$ — correct.
  • $m_1 = m_2 = m$: $a = 0$, $T = mg$ — equilibrium (no motion).
  • $m_1 \ll m_2$: $a \to g$, $T \to 2m_1 g$ — heavy mass nearly free-falls, light mass pulled up with ~$g$.
  • $m_2 \ll m_1$: $a \to -g$ (roles reverse) — formula still holds if $m_1>m_2$.

Your solution already passed the $Mg-mg = (M+m)a$ step correctly — this confirms no algebraic error.

Final Result

$$ |a_1| = |a_2| = \frac{m_2-m_1}{m_1+m_2}\,g \;\; \text{( $m_2$ down, $m_1$ up )} $$

$$ T = \frac{2m_1 m_2}{m_1+m_2}\,g $$

With $g \approx 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$ (or $10\,\text{m/s}^2$ for estimation). If you provide numerical $m_1,m_2$, substitute directly.

Common Pitfall Addressed

Writing $a = \dfrac{T-mg}{m}$ alone is not a solution for $a$ (circular, since $T$ unknown). You correctly eliminated $T$ by equating $T=m(a+g)$ with $Mg-T=Ma$ — that's the key step.
